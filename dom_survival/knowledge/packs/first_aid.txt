# First Aid

## CPR (Adult)
1. Check for responsiveness — tap shoulders, shout
2. Call for help or have someone call emergency services
3. Place heel of hand on center of chest (between nipples)
4. Compress hard and fast — at least 2 inches deep, 100-120 per minute
5. Allow full chest recoil between compressions
6. If trained: 30 compressions, 2 rescue breaths (head tilt, chin lift)
7. If untrained: hands-only CPR (continuous compressions) is effective
8. Continue until emergency services arrive or person recovers

## Severe Bleeding Control
1. Apply direct pressure with cloth or hands — this is the #1 priority
2. If blood soaks through, add more material on top — do not remove first layer
3. Apply pressure to the artery closest to the heart (pressure points)
4. Arm: press inside of upper arm. Leg: press in groin area
5. Tourniquet as last resort: 2-3 inches above wound, tighten until bleeding stops
6. Mark the time of tourniquet application — document for medical professionals
7. Keep victim warm and still, elevate injured limb if possible

## Burns
- First degree: red, painful, no blisters — cool water 10-20 minutes, aloe vera
- Second degree: red, painful, blisters forming — cool water, do not pop blisters, cover loosely
- Third degree: white/charring, may be painless — call emergency services immediately, cover with clean cloth
- Never use ice, butter, or toothpaste on burns
- Cool running water is the best immediate treatment — not cold water
- Remove jewelry and tight clothing before swelling begins

## Fractures
- Suspected fracture: do not move the injured area
- Splint in the position found — do not try to straighten
- Immobilize joint above AND below the fracture
- Check circulation below the fracture (pulse, sensation, color)
- Improvised splints: sticks, rolled magazines, cardboard, even a pillow
- Pad the splint for comfort and to prevent pressure sores
- Seek medical attention — all fractures need professional treatment

## Choking (Conscious Adult)
1. Ask "Are you choking?" — if they cannot speak, cough, or breathe:
2. Stand behind the person, wrap arms around waist
3. Make a fist with one hand, place thumb side against abdomen above navel
4. Grasp fist with other hand, thrust inward and upward (Heimlich maneuver)
5. Repeat until object is expelled or person becomes unconscious
6. If unconscious: begin CPR, check mouth for visible object before breaths

## Heat Exhaustion vs Heat Stroke
Heat Exhaustion: heavy sweating, cold/pale/clammy skin, fast/weak pulse, nausea, fainting
- Move to cool place, loosen clothing, cool wet cloths, sip water
- If vomiting occurs, seek medical attention

Heat Stroke: high body temperature (103°F+), hot/red/dry skin, fast/strong pulse, confusion
- THIS IS A MEDICAL EMERGENCY — call 911 immediately
- Move to cooler area, cool rapidly with any means available (water, ice packs, fans)
- Do NOT give fluids if person is unconscious

## Poisoning
- Do NOT induce vomiting unless specifically told to by poison control
- Try to identify the substance — keep container, plant, or sample
- Call Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222 (US) or local equivalent
- If on skin: remove contaminated clothing, rinse with water 15-20 minutes
- If in eyes: flush with clean water for 15-20 minutes, hold eyelids open
- If inhaled: move to fresh air immediately
- Note time of exposure and symptoms for medical professionals

## Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis)
Severe signs: difficulty breathing, swelling of face/throat, rapid pulse, dizziness, hives
- Use epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) if available — inject into outer thigh
- Can repeat after 5-15 minutes if no improvement
- Call emergency services immediately
- Lay person flat, elevate legs (unless breathing is difficult — then sit up)
- If person has known allergies, help them use their own medication
